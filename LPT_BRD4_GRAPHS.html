01 — What was asked

One seed, two independent retrieval surfaces

BRD4 is the fourth-largest hub in the shipped corpus (22 partners, 258 co-mentions). It is also in DepMap, so both surfaces have something to say about it. Neither graph below involved a language model: the corpus graph is built from extracted protein_pair claims, and the co-essentiality graph from Pearson correlations over CRISPR gene-effect profiles. Both are drawn by LPT's single network renderer, so the visual grammar is identical and the two pictures are directly comparable.

02 — Literature

What the corpus says BRD4 works with

The neighbourhood is recognisably BRD4's published biology: CDK9 (the P-TEFb kinase BRD4 recruits to pause-released polymerase), RNA Pol II, MYC (the canonical BET-inhibitor readout, and the heaviest edge at 10 co-mentions), CRBN (cereblon — the E3 ligase handle every BRD4 degrader is built on), the nuclear receptors AR and ERα, and the paralog BRD9.

Two artefacts visible in the picture, not hidden

RNAPOLII and POLII are separate nodes, each with its own edge to BRD4 (6 and 5 co-mentions). They are the same molecule under two spellings the identifier normaliser does not collapse — the same alias fragmentation the maintainers documented for YAP1 vs YAP. And ERα is stored with a Greek capital alpha. A map drawn on raw graph nodes shows this; the persisted edge index, which collapses by approved gene symbol, would not.

Two of these edges carry a measured affinity

An edge is not only a count. Where a curated finding recorded a binding constant, the edge carries it — and in this neighbourhood two do, both visible on hover:

Coverage is thin and worth stating plainly: 1,966 of the graph's 26,490 edges (7.4%) carry any affinity — 1,503 a Kd, 575 a Ki. An edge without one means the corpus recorded no number, never that the interaction is weak.

Three things the number on an edge does not tell you

"Tightest" is a best case. The edge stores the strongest value across every finding for that pair, so an edge backed by four papers shows the most favourable one. The full list of anchors, each with its own DOI and page reference, is what the graph tools hand a model — the reduction to a single minimum exists only for the drawn map.

The field can be looser than the assay. The BRD4–CDK9 finding's own experimental_context reads "IC50 in nM", but the extraction schema offers only Kd and Ki, so an IC50 was recorded as a Ki. The context string is the ground truth about what was measured; the number alone is not.

It belongs to the pair, not to a construct. AR–BRD4's 70 nM is an NTD-to-BD1/BD2 domain measurement. The claim text says so; the edge does not.

03 — Co-essentiality

What cells say BRD4 is needed alongside

Two protein complexes fall out of the correlations without being told to:

  • SAGA — TAF5L, TAF6L, TADA2B, SUPT20H, SGF29 form the densest clique in the graph, at r = 0.62 to 0.82 among themselves. Those five are literally subunits of the same coactivator complex, recovered from nothing but the pattern of which cell lines die when each is knocked out.
  • Mediator — MED19–MED24 at r = 0.574 and MED19–MED15 at 0.503. BRD4's own two strongest correlates anywhere are MED19 (0.273) and MED24 (0.270).
The one negative edge is the interesting one

Of the 66 correlated pairs exactly one is negative: BRD4 – HEXIM1, r = −0.214. HEXIM1 is the subunit of the 7SK snRNP that sequesters and inhibits P-TEFb — the same CDK9-containing kinase that Graph A shows BRD4 recruiting. The two surfaces have independently converged on opposite ends of one mechanism: the literature graph names the activator BRD4 delivers, and the essentiality graph names its endogenous brake, with the sign to match. Neither graph contains both.

The two neighbourhoods do not merely differ in ranking — they do not intersect at all, and the literature edges that can be scored against DepMap are all essentially uncorrelated. A target-selection step reading only one surface would see a coherent, well-evidenced picture and never learn that it was seeing half of one.

That is why the pipeline exposes both and uses them for different jobs. The literature graph supports "is this interaction real, and who has drugged it" — the question the pathway and literature stages ask. Co-essentiality supports "is this dependency load-bearing in actual cells, independent of how fashionable the protein is" — which is the wildcard track's novelty triage, and why the edge index drops a co-mentioned pair that DepMap actively says is uncorrelated rather than keeping it at a default weight.

What this example does not show

Correlation over essentiality profiles is not physical interaction: SAGA subunits correlate because knocking any of them out cripples the same complex, not because they were observed to touch. The sign convention is equally slippery — a negative r can mean compensation, a synthetic-lethal-style relationship, or an inhibitor/target pair as with HEXIM1, and magnitude is the reliable signal. And BRD4 is a best case for both surfaces; a protein with two corpus papers and no DepMap entry produces two nearly empty graphs, which is itself the answer the novelty triage is looking for.

05 — Reproducing it

The exact calls

Both graphs come from the tool surface the skills use, so the same neighbourhoods are reachable conversationally over MCP or from any skill that carries the graph tools.

# Graph A — literature interaction neighbourhood (Cytoscape JSON, then render)
export_subgraph(["BRD4"], Path("data/fingerprints"), "brd4.cyjs",
                depth=1, max_nodes=40, with_depmap=True, human_only=True)
# keep the seed + top 12 partners by co-mention, plus every edge among them

# Graph B — DepMap co-essentiality neighbourhood
find_cocorrelated_genes("BRD4", top_k=12, min_abs_r=0.2, min_n=100)
# then correlation_for_pair() over all 78 pairs; draw every BRD4 edge,
# and partner-partner edges only above |r| = 0.50

# both rendered by the one canonical renderer
network_svg.render_svg(nodes, edges, meta, width=760, height=460)

Runtime is dominated by loading the 421 MB DepMap matrix once (~16 s, 18,531 genes × 1,208 cell lines, 90 MB as float32); the graph itself builds from 14,514 fingerprints in under a second and is cached by file mtime. The layout is a seeded Fruchterman-Reingold with no RNG, so re-running this produces a byte-identical picture.
