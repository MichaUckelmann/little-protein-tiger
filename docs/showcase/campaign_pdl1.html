SCALE_UP — the trial justified the full campaign

Stage 1 · target intel

Which structure, and which face of it?

The pipeline was given one instruction: Design binders against PD-L1 to block its interaction with PD-1. It resolved PD-L1 to UniProt Q9NZQ7, pulled every IgV-domain complex it could find, and measured all 9 of them — buried surface area, interface residue count, hydrogen bonds, hydrophobic fraction, resolution — before choosing. It settled on 8ZNL, with chain B as the target (PD-L1, 114 modelled residues) and chain A as the partner it had to displace — the PD-L1 de novo binder.

8ZNL provides a high-resolution (1.77 Å) template of a validated de novo miniprotein binding the PD-1-competitive IgV face with high BSA (1844 Å²) and balanced hydrophobicity (phi = 0.39). — binder-target-intel, 20_target_intel.md

That quote contains a checkable error, and the record catches it. 8ZNL is not the highest-resolution entry in the table: row 2 is 8AOK at 1.60 Å, against 8ZNL's 1.77 Å. The choice still stands on the reasons that did the work — largest BSA (1,844 Å²), 31 interface residues, 17 H-bonds, hydrophobic fraction 0.39 — but the superlative is wrong, and it is only visible because candidates/candidates.md is kept alongside the prose written from it.

Four alternatives were rejected in writing, each in the stage's own words. 7CZD (1.64 Å) — “Alternative high-affinity VHH interface.” 8AOK (1.60 Å) — “VHH6 interface with lower hydrophobic fraction (phi = 0.22) compared to 8ZNL (phi = 0.39).” 5JDS (1.70 Å) — “Nanobody KN035 complex.” 8AOM (2.20 Å) — “Extracellular ectodomain complex with sparse hydrogen bonds (3) and lower interface quality.” The stage also proposed a second designable site — vhh_czd_epitope on 7CZD, an existing de novo mini-binder at the same IgV face. This run took the first; a run today would pass --trial-sites 2 and settle it on measured yield instead of argument, one trial per site under binder/sites/<site_id>/.

Stage 2 · interface analysis

10 residues, and the evidence for each

The interface stage does not accept the textbook answer. It reads the actual residue at every position in the downloaded structure and computes per-residue buried area, then ranks contiguous patches by how designable they are. Two regions came back: the Central β-Sheet Hydrophobic & Aromatic Core (rated excellent) and the F-G Loop / Strand Basic & Aromatic Anchor (rated excellent).

It also grounded the choice in the corpus, and the run records how far that went: 3 of 3 cited DOIs were found in the local literature database — mutagenesis and inhibitor work on this exact face, led by 10.7554/eLife.22889. "Cited" and "checked against the corpus" are different claims, and the pipeline only makes the second because it is the one it can verify.

The textbook answer would have been wrong on this entry. The PD-L1 hotspot every review names is Tyr56 — and on 8ZNL, chain B residue 56 is a valine. Asked to analyse this structure, the interface stage has previously returned the canonical numbering verbatim: residues that are real and correctly numbered in a different PD-L1 crystal form. Two guards run before any trim is built — one reads the actual residue name at every position in the downloaded file, the other confirms by sequence identity to UniProt that the target chain is the target. Both passed here, and the aromatic anchors this run chose are Tyr124 and Tyr57, which are the tyrosines 8ZNL actually has.

Stage 4 · pilot and calibration

Measure the hit rate before buying the GPU time

PD-L1 needed no trimming — 114 modelled residues in, 114 out, 1 contiguous segment, all 10 hotspots retained; deciding a target is already the right size is as much that stage's job as cutting one down. The contig handed to RFD3, 70-86,/0,B20-133, asks for a binder of 70–86 residues against it.

A pilot of 100 backbones proved the machinery end to end in 66 minutes. Then the calibration stage ran the experiment that actually decides the campaign: 580 backbones diffused, 479 of them cleared the prefilter (82.6%), and each survivor was threaded with 4 sequences — 1,916 refolds. It then counted how many cleared the success bar rather than assuming a rate.

Designs surviving at each ipTM bar

The requested bar was ipTM > 0.7. The calibration stage walks the bar ladder strictest-first and takes the hardest rung that still clears five hits and still fits the budget — so it raised the bar to 0.85 by itself, and sized the campaign to that. Only ever upward: it will not quietly make a campaign easier than you asked for.

Backbone hit rate, 95% Wilson interval

33 of 479 backbones produced an excellent design. The campaign is sized on the pessimistic end of that interval, not the point estimate: 4,043 refolds, ~13.9 GPU-hours, ~3.8 GB — well inside the budget. Verdict: SCALE_UP.

This gate is also where the campaign becomes resumable. The verdict, the batch count and the raised bar are all persisted to calibration/calibration.json, so a process that dies overnight and restarts at --start-from production re-derives the measured plan instead of falling back to the config default — which here would have been an order of magnitude more GPU time than the trial said was needed.

Stage 5 · production

What 13.7 GPU-hours produced

Production funnel

708 designs cleared every hard gate — 17.8% of the refolds, spread across 390 distinct backbones. The chart shows which gate was the first to reject each design that failed.

Dock RMSD sits first in that order and absorbs the largest share (1,577 refolds), but the order is what makes it look decisive: 89.2% of those (1,407) had ipTM below the 0.5 gate as well, and would have been dropped by the confidence gate a line later. The honest reading of this run is the opposite of a warning: 95% of refolds with ipTM > 0.7 (759 of 802) are docked on the intended patch. PD-L1 is an easy docking target.

The gate still earns its place, twice over. It caught the 43 designs here that RF3 was confident about and had put in the wrong place — exactly the failure ipTM cannot see, because RF3 cannot be given a docked pose at inference and only reports confidence in the interface it chose for itself. And PD-L1 is the easy case: of designs with ipTM > 0.7, only 45% (8TAC) and 8% (CD79b) were docked on target. On those campaigns the gate is not a formality, it is most of the answer.

Refolds dropped by the first gate they failed

These survivor counts are historical. This run gated at hotspot_engagement ≥ 1 — every declared hotspot contacted. config.yaml now sets 0.75, because requiring all of them rejects refolds for missing residues the RFD3 design never targeted itself. Re-gating this run's own refold_scores.csv at 0.75 gives 708 survivors across 390 backbones, not 708/390. The funnel and the chart above show what this campaign actually decided, at the threshold it decided with.

Stage 6 · scoring and ranking

The designs that came out

Survivors are ranked on a composite of ipSAE, dock RMSD, pLDDT, ipTM, interface PAE, epitope recall and hotspot engagement, then diversified so the top-K is not 20 variations of one backbone. Rosetta relax and InterfaceAnalyzer run after the gates, on 300 of the 708 survivors, and enter the composite only — a mis-docked pose is still a physical pose, and Rosetta will happily return well-defined, meaningless numbers for it.

Independent check · outside the run

Would it actually get in PD-1's way?

Nothing in this campaign ever saw PD-1. The epitope was chosen from a crystallised binder complex, and the designs were folded against PD-L1 alone. So the human PD-1/PD-L1 complex — PDB 4ZQK, which played no part in the run — is a genuinely independent way to ask whether the pipeline aimed at the right patch.

This check is computed at build time, not by hand. The earlier version of this page reported a ChimeraX session run after the campaign finished and said so, because an unversioned session cannot be extracted and must not be presented as though the pipeline produced it. It is now done in code from the run's own rank-1 refold, so it goes through the same facts/ snapshot as every other number here and moves as a reviewable diff. 4ZQK is still not part of the run — that is the point of it — but the measurement is now reproducible: the refold→author residue offset is derived and then checked against all 10 hotspot names before a single contact is counted, because a wrong offset reports contacts for arbitrary residues and looks perfectly fine.

Superposing 4ZQK's PD-L1 onto the campaign's own copy puts both partners in one frame: 0.87 Å over 113 Cα at 100.0% sequence identity, so the two really are the same protein and the comparison is fair. Then it is a matter of counting which PD-L1 residues each partner touches, at a 4.5 Å heavy-atom cutoff.

80%of PD-1's footprint covered
12/15shared contact residues
6/10chosen hotspots are real PD-1 contacts
0.87 Åsuperposition RMSD

This is a geometric argument, not a measured one. Occupying 80% of a footprint says a binder is in the way; it says nothing about whether it out-competes PD-1, which depends on affinities neither measured here nor predictable from these structures. The 3 residues PD-1 uses and the design misses — 20, 24 and 27 — sit at the edge of the interface. Read this as evidence the pipeline aimed where it said it would, not as evidence of a blocker.

What it cost

Three LLM stages, 21.4 GPU-hours

Only three stages of this campaign involved a language model at all: target intel, interface, binder summary — 3 billed API calls between them, because an agentic stage bills once per turn that reaches the model and the interface stage took 1. Everything else — trimming, spec building, campaign planning, gating, scoring, ranking — is deterministic Python. The LLM spend was $0.18 against a $10 hard cap, itemised per stage and per model in an append-only ledger.

That headline is now a ceiling. This run billed gemini-3.7-flash; every pipeline stage today defaults to gemini (gemini-3.7-flash), roughly four times cheaper on input — a change made because safety-classifier refusals on structural-biology prompts were costing real money for no output, not to save the fifty cents.
