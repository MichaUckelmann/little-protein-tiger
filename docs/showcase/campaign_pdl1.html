Designing a mini-protein that blocks PD-1 from reaching PD-L1

SCALE_UP — the trial justified the full campaign

Stage 1 · target intel

Which structure, and which face of it?

The pipeline was given one instruction: Design binders against PD-L1 to block its interaction with PD-1. It resolved PD-L1 to UniProt Q9NZQ7, pulled every IgV-domain complex it could find, and measured all 9 of them — buried surface area, interface residue count, hydrogen bonds, hydrophobic fraction, resolution — before choosing. It settled on 7CZD, with chain B as the target (PD-L1, 117 modelled residues) and chain A as the partner it had to displace — the anti-PD-L1 VHH.

largest BSA, most complete interface residue coverage (44), strong H-bond network (30) and good hydrophobicity (0.34) at the highest resolution (1.64 Å) among all IgV-domain complexes in the table — binder-target-intel, 20_target_intel.md

That quote contains a checkable error, and the record catches it. 7CZD is not the highest-resolution entry in the table: row 2 is 8AOK at 1.60 Å, against 7CZD's 1.64 Å. The choice still stands on the reasons that did the work — largest BSA (2,449 Å²), 44 interface residues, 30 H-bonds, hydrophobic fraction 0.34 — but the superlative is wrong, and it is only visible because candidates/candidates.md is kept alongside the prose written from it.

Four alternatives were rejected in writing, each in the stage's own words. 8AOK (1.60 Å) — “second-largest BSA and most H-bonds, but low hydrophobic fraction (0.22) suggests a flatter, more polar epitope, harder to reproduce with a de novo binder.” 7C88 (2.00 Å) — “clinically validated (JS003) but smaller interface (1347 Å²) than 7CZD/8AOK.” 8AOM (2.20 Å) — “full-ectodomain chain (222 aa, exceeds trim budget), extremely hydrophobic with almost no H-bonds (3).” 7SJQ (2.00 Å) — “smaller interface (1159 Å²) from a cystine-dense peptide scaffold.” The stage also proposed a second designable site — denovo_precedent on 8ZNL, an existing de novo mini-binder at the same IgV face. This run took the first; a run today would pass --trial-sites 2 and settle it on measured yield instead of argument, one trial per site under binder/sites/<site_id>/.

Stage 2 · interface analysis

9 residues, and the evidence for each

The interface stage does not accept the textbook answer. It reads the actual residue at every position in the downloaded structure and computes per-residue buried area, then ranks contiguous patches by how designable they are. Two regions came back: the C-terminal F/G-strand (rated excellent) and the CC′/FG-loop (rated good).

It also grounded the choice in the corpus: a PD-L1-mimicking peptide built around exactly Tyr56, Arg113, Ala121, Asp122 and Tyr123 binds PD-1 at Kd ≈ 1.38 µM — one citation checked, one verified against the local literature database.

Asked to analyse this exact structure on an earlier occasion, the stage assigned target_chain=A and wrote its hotspots on the anti-PD-L1 VHH's own CDR loop — real, correctly-numbered residues on the wrong molecule, which hotspot checking alone cannot catch. Two guards now run before any trim is built: one reads the real residue name at each position, the other confirms the target chain is the target by sequence identity to UniProt. Both passed here, on chain B.

Stage 4 · pilot and calibration

Measure the hit rate before buying the GPU time

PD-L1 needed no trimming — 117 modelled residues in, 117 out, 1 contiguous segment, all 9 hotspots retained; deciding a target is already the right size is as much that stage's job as cutting one down. The contig handed to RFD3, 70-86,/0,B18-134, asks for a binder of 70–86 residues against it.

A pilot of 100 backbones proved the machinery end to end in 52 minutes. Then the calibration stage ran the experiment that actually decides the campaign: 580 backbones diffused, 358 of them cleared the prefilter (61.7%), and each survivor was threaded with 4 sequences — 1,432 refolds. It then counted how many cleared the success bar rather than assuming a rate.

Designs surviving at each ipTM bar

The requested bar was ipTM > 0.7. The calibration stage walks the bar ladder strictest-first and takes the hardest rung that still clears five hits and still fits the budget — so it raised the bar to 0.85 by itself, and sized the campaign to that. Only ever upward: it will not quietly make a campaign easier than you asked for.

Backbone hit rate, 95% Wilson interval

19 of 358 backbones produced an excellent design. The campaign is sized on the pessimistic end of that interval, not the point estimate: 5,842 refolds, ~17.8 GPU-hours, ~14.6 GB — well inside the budget. Verdict: SCALE_UP.

This gate is also where the campaign becomes resumable. The verdict, the batch count and the raised bar are all persisted to calibration/calibration.json, so a process that dies overnight and restarts at --start-from production re-derives the measured plan instead of falling back to the config default — which here would have been an order of magnitude more GPU time than the trial said was needed.

Stage 5 · production

What 20.6 GPU-hours produced

Production funnel

715 designs cleared every hard gate — 12.3% of the refolds, spread across 462 distinct backbones. The chart shows which gate was the first to reject each design that failed.

Dock RMSD sits first in that order and absorbs the largest share (2,464 refolds), but the order is what makes it look decisive: 90.6% of those (2,232) had ipTM below the 0.5 gate as well, and would have been dropped by the confidence gate a line later. The honest reading of this run is the opposite of a warning: 95% of refolds with ipTM > 0.7 (975 of 1,026) are docked on the intended patch. PD-L1 is an easy docking target.

The gate still earns its place, twice over. It caught the 51 designs here that RF3 was confident about and had put in the wrong place — exactly the failure ipTM cannot see, because RF3 cannot be given a docked pose at inference and only reports confidence in the interface it chose for itself. And PD-L1 is the easy case: of designs with ipTM > 0.7, only 45% (8TAC) and 8% (CD79b) were docked on target. On those campaigns the gate is not a formality, it is most of the answer.

Refolds dropped by the first gate they failed

These survivor counts are historical. This run gated at hotspot_engagement ≥ 1 — every declared hotspot contacted. config.yaml now sets 0.75, because requiring all of them rejects refolds for missing residues the RFD3 design never targeted itself. Re-gating this run's own refold_scores.csv at 0.75 gives 752 survivors across 488 backbones, not 715/462. The funnel and the chart above show what this campaign actually decided, at the threshold it decided with.

The designs that came out

Survivors are ranked on a composite of ipSAE, dock RMSD, pLDDT, ipTM, interface PAE, epitope recall and hotspot engagement, then diversified so the top-K is not 20 variations of one backbone. Independent check · manual, outside the run

Would it actually get in PD-1's way?

Nothing in this campaign ever saw PD-1. The epitope was chosen from a nanobody complex, and the designs were folded against PD-L1 alone. So the human PD-1/PD-L1 complex — PDB 4ZQK, which played no part in the run — is a genuinely independent way to ask whether the pipeline aimed at the right patch.

Everything in this section was done by hand. It is a UCSF ChimeraX superposition run after the campaign finished, not a pipeline stage: unlike every other number on this page it is not extracted from projects/pdl1_e2e, and it is not reproducible by re-running the campaign.

Superposing 4ZQK's PD-L1 onto the campaign's own copy puts both partners in one frame: 0.83 Å over 115 Cα at 99.1% sequence identity, so the two really are the same protein and the comparison is fair. Then it is a matter of counting which PD-L1 residues each partner touches, at a 4.5 Å heavy-atom cutoff.

78%of PD-1's footprint covered
14/18shared contact residues

This is a geometric argument, not a measured one. Occupying 78% of a footprint says a binder is in the way; it says nothing about whether it out-competes PD-1, which depends on affinities neither measured here nor predictable from these structures. The 4 residues PD-1 uses and the design misses — 19, 23, 26 and 124 — sit at the edge of the interface. Read this as evidence the pipeline aimed where it said it would, not as evidence of a blocker.

What it cost

Three LLM stages, 26.4 GPU-hours

Only three stages of this campaign involved a language model at all: target intel, interface, binder summary — 5 billed API calls between them, because an agentic stage bills once per turn that reaches the model and the interface stage took 3. Everything else — trimming, spec building, campaign planning, gating, scoring, ranking — is deterministic Python. The LLM spend was $2.12 against a $10 hard cap, itemised per stage and per model in an append-only ledger.

That headline is now a ceiling. This run billed claude-haiku-4-5 and claude-sonnet-5; every pipeline stage today defaults to gemini (gemini-3.7-flash), roughly four times cheaper on input — a change made because safety-classifier refusals on structural-biology prompts were costing real money for no output, not to save the fifty cents.
