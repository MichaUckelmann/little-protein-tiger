One sentence about a disease, twenty designed binders out

No target was named. The result, first

Production funnel

For scale, the PD-L1 campaign returned 12.3% from 4.3× the refolds. Rosetta relax and InterfaceAnalyzer ran afterwards on the survivors and enter the composite only; a mis-docked pose is still a physical pose, and Rosetta will happily score one.

The gates are not optional, and the two doing the most work are the two a confidence score cannot replace. RF3 cannot be given a docked pose at inference, so ipTM reports confidence in whatever interface the model chose for itself — ranking on it alone selects confidently mis-docked binders. binder_rmsd_dock is the gate that catches them.

The failure mode here is different from PD-L1's, and more tractable. There, dock-RMSD did the killing — designs confidently docked in the wrong place. Here 84.8% pass dock-RMSD and the leading killer is steric clash. The designs are landing on the right face in the right pose and being rejected on packing.

Stage 0 · pathway-expert

Three candidate interactions, ranked by the evidence behind them

The pathway stage does not return one answer. It returns tiered options, each with the evidence it rests on and the reason it might fail — and the tiers are a closed set, so "this is a guess" cannot be quietly promoted to "this is validated" by confident prose.

The uncertainty it flagged on the winner — TAZ co-activator redundancy — is independently visible in the DepMap data behind the corpus-explorer page: YAP1 and WWTR1 (TAZ) are co-essential at r = +0.02, meaning losing one does not substitute for the other. Two different sources, same warning.

Stages 1–2 · literature and structure

Is there mutagenesis behind the interface, or only a picture of it?

A large buried surface means nothing on its own. The literature stage went looking for the numbers that say which residues carry the binding energy, and came back with a native affinity of Kd ≈ 18 nM, alanine-scanning hotspots at F69, R89, L91, F95, and precedent for peptidomimetic druggability — every claim carrying its DOI. All 8 of the 8 citations it made were checked against the local corpus and found there; none were invented.

The structure stage then worked on the coordinates rather than the prose. On PDB 3KYS it measured 3,402 Å² of buried surface, identified chain A as TEAD1 by synonym match with expected target TEAD1 against the requested target, and selected 12 hotspots: Gln246, Asp249, Lys274, Trp276, Phe314, Val318, Tyr346, Phe350, Lys353, Val366, His404, Tyr406. Those names are not taken on trust: before any spec is built, each is read back out of the downloaded structure at its own auth_seq_id, and a mismatch halts the run.

Stage 4 · calibration

The campaign sized itself, then raised its own bar

A 100-backbone pilot proved the machinery in 1.93 h. Calibration then ran the experiment that decides the campaign: 481 backbones, 1,924 refolds, counting how many designs actually cleared the success bar rather than assuming a rate.

It also cleared a first for this pipeline. TEAD1 needed no trimming — all 207 modelled residues were already inside budget — but 3KYS has a disordered stretch with no density, so the target reaches RFD3 in 3 pieces: 70-86,/0,A195-229,A239-343,A345-411. The target is fixed conditioning rather than something the model builds, so the gaps are not its problem; what they do change is the prefilter's chain-break count, which is derived from the segment number rather than hardcoded. A hardcoded limit would have rejected every design. It held — 82.9% of calibration backbones cleared the prefilter, and 86.2% in production — and the refolded target superposes onto the deposited chain at 0.11 Å median Cα RMSD across the top 20, so the pieces have not drifted apart.

The measured backbone hit rate was 18.50% (89 of 481, 95% Wilson interval 15.29–22.22%) — 3.5× PD-L1's 5.31%. The requested bar was ipTM > 0.7; because the trial supported a harder one within budget, the stage raised it to 0.85 by itself and sized to that. It only ever moves the bar up.

Sizing is done on the pessimistic end of the interval, never the point estimate: ~1,308 refolds, ~3.8 GPU-h, ~3.3 GB — and the same arithmetic places the work, comparing 3.8 h on this workstation's single GPU against the 48 h local budget. Verdict: SCALE_UP, local.

This is the run's one pause point, and the only place a human intervened. The plan is persisted to calibration.json as it is made, so resuming with --start-from production in a fresh process — the normal case for a multi-day campaign — re-derives this sizing and this local-vs-cluster choice instead of falling back to the config default.

The trial cost more GPU time than the campaign it sized — 11.53 h against 8.60 h — because raising the bar to ipTM > 0.85 is also what let production be small. That is the trade the gate exists to make: spend measurably, once, instead of guessing at scale. This campaign also produced the pipeline's hotspot_engagement >= 0.75 rule. Calibration measured its rate under the stricter hotspot_engagement >= 1 — every declared hotspot contacted — and its own attrition showed that gate rejecting refolds for missing residues the backbone they came from had never touched. Production ran at hotspot_engagement >= 0.75, which is now the default.

The same question, twice

What changed when the PPI track was rewired

An earlier run asked this pipeline the same thing about mesothelioma and reached the same target — YAP1 / TEAD1 on 3KYS — through the older BoltzGen path. Putting them side by side is the clearest measure of what the foundry bridge bought.

BoltzGen path (archived)

100 → 100 → 20
designs → survivors → top-K

Every design survived every filter; drop reasons: (none). A funnel that rejects nothing has not demonstrated that its filters work — and at 100 designs there is no measured hit rate to size anything from. Best design: a 15-mer at ipTM 0.591.

foundry bridge (this run)

392 → 1,352 → 317 → 20
backbones → refolds → gated → top-K

Sized from a measured 18.5% hit rate with a confidence interval, gated on 8 criteria with the attrition recorded per criterion, and ranked on a composite including Rosetta terms. Best design: a 86-mer at ipTM 0.914, dock-RMSD 0.63 Å.

This run is what made the bridge the default: design.backend is now foundry, so a PPI-discovered target hands off to the same RFD3→solubleMPNN→RF3 stage machine --workflow binder uses. Until this campaign finished, that hand-off was verified only by unit tests with the GPU stubbed out — the pipeline's own documentation listed "no real end-to-end GPU run has proven the bridge" as a known gap. BoltzGen is not deprecated: --design-engine boltzgen still selects it, and it is required for cyclic peptides, which RFD3 cannot build — the archived run above was a cyclic_peptide campaign, which is exactly why its designs are 15-mers.

What it cost, and what it is not

Four model stages

Four LLM stages in the entire run — binder_summary, literature, pathway, structure — for $0.79 against a $5.00 cap on gemini-3.7-flash. Each is a whole agentic tool-use loop rather than a single call: pathway alone billed 483k uncached input tokens. Everything between them is deterministic Python — the trim, the spec, campaign planning, gating, scoring, ranking — and the GPU did 1.93 h of pilot, 11.53 h of calibration and 8.60 h of production, 22.1 h in all.

Every design here is an unvalidated computational hypothesis. Nothing has been expressed, purified or measured. A high ipTM says a folding model is confident about an interface it chose; a low dock-RMSD says the binder is where it was asked to be. Neither is an affinity, and neither is evidence that this would disrupt YAP1–TEAD1 in a cell. Anyone synthesising one of these sequences is responsible for screening it — see docs/responsible-use.md.
