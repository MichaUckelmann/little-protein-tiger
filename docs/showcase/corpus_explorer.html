Little Protein Tiger · corpus-explorer

Ask 14,517 papers a question and get a target map back

The corpus is not a search box. It is a curated store of structured findings — every claim carrying its own page reference — wired to an interaction graph and to DepMap co-essentiality. It is also small and lopsided: 14,517 papers weighted toward chromatin, histone chaperones and structural biology, and silent on most of biology. This is one real session against it: one question, 4 model calls, 65 seconds.

What one paper becomes

A finding, not a snippet

Curation turns each paper into structured JSON against a fixed schema. Units are normalised at extraction time — affinities are floats in Molar, never nM or µM — organisms become NCBI taxonomy ids, and study categories come from a closed enum. That is what makes "find every Kd tighter than 100 nM for this pair" a query rather than a reading exercise.

The rule that matters most is the last field: every claim carries a source_span. A finding that cannot say which page and paragraph it came from is rejected by the consumers downstream. There is no way to produce a confident-sounding number here without a pointer to where it was read.

This particular record is why the map below has a VGLL4 node at all: a natural TEAD1 ligand with a measured 3.1 nM Kd, curated out of 10.1016/j.devcel.2016.09.005 by gemini-3.1-flash-lite-preview in 2026-05 — provenance is stored per file, so you can always ask which model read what.

NF2 (Merlin) is inactivated in 30–40% of malignant pleural mesothelioma (MPM) cases (10.1111/jcmm.18330). When NF2 is lost, it can no longer relay contact-inhibition signals through the Hippo kinase cascade (MST1/2 → LATS1/2), so YAP1 and TAZ (WWTR1) accumulate in the nucleus and drive a TEAD1-4-dependent pro-proliferative transcriptional program (CYR61, CTGF as canonical readouts). — corpus-explorer, verbatim from the session transcript

Every claim in the written answer is either cited or explicitly marked [uncited]. The skill labels its own unsupported statements rather than letting them blend in with the sourced ones — this answer carries 4 such marks, every one of them a claim the model knew from training and could not find in the corpus.

The finding that needed both sources

LATS1 and LATS2 are essentially not co-essential with each other (r = +0.04) — the signature of paralog buffering. The signal only appears against the upstream node: NF2–LATS2 reaches r = +0.55. The same pattern holds for YAP1 and its paralog: YAP1–WWTR1 (TAZ) is r = +0.02 — knocking out one does not substitute for the other, which is exactly why a TAZ-blind binder can be bypassed. And NF2–YAP1 is negative, r = -0.24: lose the brake, gain the dependency.

Literature alone would have called LATS1/2 one module. DepMap alone would have called them unrelated. Neither source answers this on its own.

novelty_signal0.05

YAP1: 482 mentions, 119 prior targeting efforts, 19 quantitative findings. The score is a saturation measure — near zero means everyone is already here. NF2 scores 0.51 on the same scale, off 115 mentions and 1 targeting effort.

That contrast is a prompt, not a recommendation. NF2 looks unexplored because it is a tumour suppressor that is lost in these tumours — there is nothing there to bind. A high novelty score says the literature is thin, and nothing about whether a target is tractable.

The literature graph is what has been written down: 22,424 proteins, 26,490 co-mention edges, rebuilt from the fingerprints on every query. The DepMap index is what CRISPR screens measured: 1,502 genes, 1,466 edges, each with a real correlation and sample size. An edge in both is a much stronger claim than an edge in either.

What is in it

One lab's reading list, and it says so

Curated papers by publication year

Top journals by curated paper count

Search indexes every hit, but only tier 1 and 2 journals are downloaded and curated — 25% of what the searches find (14,693 of 57,907). That gate is the single most consequential knob in the whole literature track, and it fails silently in one specific way: journal matching is exact against a normalised name, so a journal spelled a way the list does not contain is a 100% exclusion with no warning.

You can see that failure mode in the chart beside this paragraph. Nat Commun and Nature Communications are the same journal, counted separately in the database, and merged into one 1,678-paper bar by this page's own extractor. The pipeline does not merge them for you — which is exactly why the behaviour is documented rather than hidden.

This corpus is weighted toward chromatin biology, histone chaperones and structural/chemical biology. It is one lab's reading list. Ask it a question outside that space and the honest answer is that it does not know — which is why the skill is required to end every answer with what the corpus does not say, and why the MCP servers are configured never to reach for these tools on their own.

You do not have to build it

The curated corpus ships as a release asset: python scripts/fetch_corpus.py installs the 14,514 fingerprints, the LanceDB vector index and the paper database, and search_corpus works immediately. No API key, no LLM spend, no days of downloading. Source PDFs are not included — they are ~95% of the corpus on disk and nothing downstream reads them — and publisher-supplied abstracts are stripped from the shipped database. Everything on this page is reproducible from that download.

An ambiguous name resolves to nothing

Names are resolved against 52,358 approved gene symbols through ordered tiers, and the resolver is allowed to refuse. A synonym that is also another gene's approved symbol is dropped at load (1,266 such links), and a synonym still claimed by several approved genes resolves to nothing rather than to the alphabetically-first claimant. NAP1 — a yeast name — comes back ambiguous_synonym, not ACOT8. The visible cost is the split hub row above; the invisible cost of the alternative is a confident answer about the wrong gene.

Where it stops

What the corpus does NOT say

The skill is required to close every answer with its own gaps, and this is that closing section verbatim — not a summary of it. For a mesothelioma question these are the first things an expert would ask about, and the corpus does not have them:

  • BAP1 and CDKN2A — both are frequently altered in MPM [uncited] and represent a major therapeutic gap in the corpus. No papers capturing PRMT5 synthetic lethality from MTAP co-deletion (common in CDKN2A-deleted mesothelioma) showed up.
  • No clinical outcome data: The corpus is biochemistry/preclinical-biased. VT3989 clinical activity is noted qualitatively; no survival curves or OS data are captured.
  • VEGF/anti-angiogenic targets, PDL1/checkpoint data: The corpus doesn't have MPM-specific checkpoint data (bevacizumab + chemotherapy, nivolumab/ipilimumab combos are the standard-of-care [uncited] but aren't captured here).
  • Mesothelin — a major clinical target in MPM (antibody-drug conjugates, CAR-T) [uncited] — doesn't appear in this corpus at all.

A retrieval system that cannot say this ends up answering from the shape of what it happens to hold. Naming BAP1, CDKN2A/MTAP–PRMT5 and mesothelin as absent is more useful here than anything the corpus did return, because it is what tells you which question to take somewhere else.
