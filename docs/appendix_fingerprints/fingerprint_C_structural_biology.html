Disease-specific tau filaments assemble via polymorphic intermediates

DOI 10.1038/s41586-023-06788-wstructural_biologyexperimental_structuralPMC10764278

Situational context hook

This study investigates the in vitro assembly mechanism of recombinant human tau (residues 297–391) into filaments associated with Alzheimer’s disease (PHFs) and chronic traumatic encephalopathy (CTE). Using time-resolved cryo-EM, the authors identified a common, transient 'First Intermediate Amyloid' (FIA) filament, which forms as a shared intermediate at 120 minutes across different reaction conditions. The FIA core comprises residues 302–316, forming a hydrophobic interface that precedes the formation of disease-specific filament structures. The study demonstrates that subsequent maturation into diverse, polymorphic J-shaped and C-shaped protofilaments is influenced by reaction conditions (magnesium vs. sodium chloride), providing structural insights into primary and secondary nucleation processes in amyloid assembly. These findings challenge the requirement of non-filamentous oligomers as the primary drivers of amyloid formation in these models.
