USP26 functions as a negative regulator of cellular reprogramming by stabilising PRC1 complex components

DOI 10.1038/s41467-017-00301-4pathway_biologyexperimental_in_vitroPMC5571198

Situational context hook

In mouse embryonic fibroblasts (MEFs) undergoing Oct4-Sox2-Klf4-cMyc (OSKM)-mediated cellular reprogramming and embryonic stem cells (ESCs) undergoing differentiation, the deubiquitinase USP26 acts as a potent negative regulator of pluripotency. Co-immunoprecipitation assays in HEK293T cells and differentiating ESCs demonstrate that USP26 physically interacts with canonical PRC1.2 complex components, specifically CBX4 and CBX6, in a RING1A-independent manner. USP26 enzymatic activity removes K48-linked polyubiquitin chains from CBX4 and CBX6, protecting them from 26S proteasomal degradation, whereas catalytic mutant USP26-C304S fails to deubiquitinate them. Accumulated CBX4 and CBX6 recruit PRC1 complexes to pluripotency gene promoters, promoting H2AK119 monoubiquitination and transcriptional repression of Sox2 and Nanog. Knockdown of Usp26 increases alkaline phosphatase-positive iPSC colony generation fourfold (~400 vs ~100 colonies) and upregulates Sox2 and Nanog expression.
