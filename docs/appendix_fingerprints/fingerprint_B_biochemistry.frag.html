Acidic C-terminal domains autoregulate the RNA chaperone Hfq

DOI 10.7554/eLife.27049biochemistryexperimental_in_vitroPMC5606850

Situational context hook

In Escherichia coli, the RNA chaperone Hfq contains an intrinsically disordered C-terminal domain (CTD) that regulates RNA binding and duplex release. Using Rosetta de novo computational modeling and in vitro fluorescence anisotropy binding assays, the acidic tip of the E. coli Hfq CTD was shown to bind specifically to basic arginine- and lysine-rich rim patches on the core Sm domain (Hfq65) with a Kd of 2.9 µM in low-salt buffer and 22 µM in high-salt buffer. This intramolecular interaction acts as a nucleic acid mimic that competitively displaces non-specific RNA and dsRNA from the rim, recycling the chaperone and preventing non-specific DNA aggregation.
